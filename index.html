About Me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Nevada, Reno. My research focuses on building robust and generalizable deep learning systems for heterogeneous visual domains, with particular emphasis on domain adaptation, medical image analysis, and computer vision.

I am interested in methods that reduce performance degradation caused by distribution shift while preserving information that is important for downstream tasks. My research uses real-world applications in medical imaging and precision agriculture to study these problems.

Abnormality detection and quantification in maize fields using selective unsupervised domain adaptation

Aminul Huq, Dimitris Zermas, George Bebis

A robust framework for detecting and quantifying maize-field abnormalities across changing field conditions. The work maps heterogeneous data into a shared domain, filters poor adaptations, and uses domain adaptation for both training-time augmentation and test-time generalization.

2025 · ISVC

Abnormality Detection in Maize Fields Using Selective Domain Adaptation–Driven Data Augmentation

Aminul Huq, Dimitris Zermas, George Bebis

Introduces selective unsupervised domain adaptation for multi-field maize imagery, using adapted data to improve training diversity while filtering low-quality translations that can hurt downstream performance.

Aminul Huq, Dimitris Zermas, George Bebis
